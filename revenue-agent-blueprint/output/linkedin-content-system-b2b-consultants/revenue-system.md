## 1. Demand Research
# Demand Research — a done-for-you LinkedIn content system for B2B consultants

## Verdict
Real demand exists, but it's crowded around one weak solution (ghostwriters) that leaves a specific, named gap: consultants pay someone to post for them and then feel disconnected from what's going out under their own name. A system that keeps the consultant's actual voice and judgment in the loop — rather than replacing it — has a real wedge. The risk is that "LinkedIn content help" alone is not differentiated; the offer has to be built around that authenticity gap specifically, not around "we'll post for you."

## Specific buyer
Not "B2B consultants" broadly — independent consultants and fractional operators (1-5 person practices, not agencies) who already know LinkedIn drives their pipeline, have tried to post consistently at least once, and stopped. They have deliverables and client calls that always outrank writing a post, so content loses by default, not by decision.

## Where they already show this pain
- Industry write-ups on LinkedIn-for-consultants content describe the core failure mode directly: sitting down to write between client calls and proposals feels like the lowest-priority task on an already packed day, which produces sporadic posting — a burst of activity followed by weeks of silence.
- The same material notes the mechanical trap: consultants treat every post as needing to be fully original, polished, and insight-dense, which makes the task exhausting almost immediately and is why most quit before consistency ever compounds into inbound leads. ([LinkedIn for Consultants & Coaches](https://searchlab.nl/en/guides/linkedin-for-consultants-coaches))
- Coverage of the LinkedIn ghostwriting market shows the adjacent solution consultants already reach for, and where it's drawing scrutiny: PR firms now run executives' LinkedIn profiles outright, and the central tension in every piece is authenticity — whether the voice and ideas are genuinely the client's or fully outsourced. ([PR firms are running execs' LinkedIn profiles](https://prosek.com/news/pr-firms-are-running-execs-linkedin-profiles-and-turning-ghostwriting-into-big/), [Is using a ghostwriter dishonest?](https://prezlo.onrender.com/forum/q/9326ced0-f319-4a70-97fd-b78b4736a891))

## What they're already trying (and where it falls short)
- **Hiring a ghostwriter.** Solves the output problem, creates a new one: the consultant stops recognizing their own voice in what's going out, and some stop checking posts before they're published — which is exactly the scenario the authenticity debate above is reacting to.
- **Content calendars / templates.** Solve the "what do I post" problem but not the "when do I actually sit down and write it" problem — the packed-week bottleneck is time and priority, not idea supply.
- **Posting in bursts when inspired.** The algorithm penalizes exactly this pattern — accounts that post once a month don't get distributed the way accounts posting 2-5x/week do, so bursty effort produces close to zero compounding return, which is likely why many consultants conclude "LinkedIn doesn't work for me."

## Their own language
- "I know I should be posting more."
- "It doesn't sound like me anymore."
- "I had a good run in January and then just... stopped."
- "I don't have time to write something original every week."
- "Does this even work, or am I wasting my time."

## Cost of inaction
Every month without consistent posting is a month the algorithm actively suppresses the account further, which means the next attempt starts from a worse baseline, not a neutral one. Meanwhile a competitor posting 2-5x/week compounds inbound visibility the entire time — so the gap isn't static, it widens.

## Risks / weak signals
"LinkedIn content" is a saturated claim — dozens of ghostwriters and template shops already pitch some version of this. The offer downstream must lead with the authenticity wedge (the consultant's real judgment stays in the loop, every post traceable to something they actually said or decided that week) rather than "we'll handle your LinkedIn," or it collapses into the same crowded, low-trust category as the ghostwriting market itself.

## 2. Offer Architecture
# Offer Architecture — a done-for-you LinkedIn content system for B2B consultants

## Positioning statement
For independent consultants who already know LinkedIn drives their pipeline but can't sustain posting once client work gets busy: a system that turns what you're already thinking and deciding each week into posts that still sound like you — not a ghostwriter who takes over your voice and leaves you checking whether you still recognize it.

## Unique mechanism
**The Weekly Capture Loop.** Instead of a ghostwriter interviewing you once and then writing from memory, a 10-minute weekly voice-note (what you worked on, what surprised you, what a client pushed back on) gets turned into that week's posts — every post traceable back to something you actually said or decided that week. The consultant never stops being the source; the system just removes the "sit down and write it from scratch" step that's the real bottleneck, not idea supply. This directly answers the authenticity gap the research surfaced: it solves the "doesn't sound like me anymore" complaint structurally, not with a promise to "capture your voice" the way every ghostwriter already claims to.

## Core offer
- Deliverable: 3 LinkedIn posts/week, drafted from the consultant's own weekly voice-note, delivered for a 48-hour review-and-edit window before anything posts.
- Format: async — one voice note in, three drafts out, consultant approves or edits in a shared doc.
- Timeframe: ongoing monthly service, first batch delivered within 5 business days of the first voice-note.
- Price: $750/month. Anchor: the research found that a single month of inconsistent posting resets the algorithm's distribution baseline, so the true cost of another dead month is pipeline, not $750 — the price is framed against that reset cost, not against a content-writer's hourly rate.

## Ladder
1. **Free:** the video — shows the gap between ghostwriting (voice lost) and a capture-based system (voice kept), live.
2. **Lead magnet — the Voice-Capture Starter Kit:** the exact weekly-capture prompt template + 3 real example posts built from a sample voice note, so a consultant can try the mechanism by hand before paying anyone. Solves "how do I even start this myself."
3. **Mid-ticket — the $750/mo done-with-you service:** solves "I tried it myself and it works but I don't have the 2 hours/week to turn the voice note into finished posts."
4. **Premium — full LinkedIn-to-pipeline build:** posts + a weekly DM/comment-to-call conversion pass for consultants who've proven the content works and want someone to also run the follow-up that turns engagement into booked calls. Solves "the posts are working but I'm not converting the engagement into clients."

| Objection | Defusing line |
|---|---|
| "I tried a ghostwriter before and it stopped sounding like me." | That's the exact problem this is built around — every post starts from your own voice note that week, not a writer's memory of an onboarding call three months ago. |
| "I don't have time for one more thing." | The only time cost is a 10-minute voice note; you're already having these thoughts during your week, this just captures them before they're gone. |
| "Does LinkedIn even work for consultants like me?" | It compounds at 2-5 posts/week and dies at 1x/month — the free Starter Kit lets you test the mechanism by hand before you pay anyone to prove that to yourself. |

## 4. Conversion Path
# Conversion Path — a done-for-you LinkedIn content system for B2B consultants

## Lead magnet
Name: The Voice-Capture Starter Kit
Contents: (1) the exact weekly voice-note prompt template used in the paid service, (2) a sample voice-note transcript, (3) the three real posts built from that transcript, with the source line highlighted in each — so the mechanism is provable, not just claimed.
Framing: infrastructure to try by hand this week, not a guide to read — "record one voice note Sunday night, have three draft posts by Monday."

## Delivery mechanics
Instant download on opt-in: a single doc with the prompt template at the top and the worked example below it. First step stated explicitly at the top of the doc: "Record your own voice note using this prompt before you do anything else — then come back and compare your three drafts to the example."

## Email sequence
1. Subject: "Here's the Starter Kit (and your first voice-note prompt)" — Direction: deliver the kit, restate the prompt inline so there's zero friction to the first action, point to the worked example — CTA: record your first voice note today.
2. Subject: "The mistake that makes this feel fake" — Direction: the #1 setup failure is writing the voice note like a LinkedIn post instead of just talking through the week out loud — stiff, pre-edited notes produce stiff, generic drafts — CTA: re-record loose if the first one felt forced.
3. Subject: "What a real week-one post looked like" — Direction: show a real before/after — the raw transcript line next to the finished post — so they know what "working" looks like before judging their own first attempt — CTA: compare their own draft against this.
4. Subject: "Ghostwriters interview you once. This happens every week." — Direction: reinforce the core mechanism difference (capture vs. one-time interview) and why that's what keeps posts sounding like them months in — CTA: none, pure reinforcement.
5. Subject: "If you want this off your plate entirely" — Direction: pitch the $750/mo done-with-you service for those who've proven the mechanism works but don't have the weekly 2 hours to turn the note into finished posts — CTA: book a call.

## Sales path logic
- Lead magnet solves: how do I even try a capture-based system instead of a ghostwriter.
- Mid-ticket solves: I proved it works by hand but don't have the weekly time to execute it.
- Premium solves: the posts are working but I'm not converting the engagement into booked calls.
